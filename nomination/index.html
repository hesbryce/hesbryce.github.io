WHY THIS APP

Six reasons, in Apple's own terms.

What follows is laid out against the criteria on developer.apple.com for getting featured. Everything claimed here is in the shipping build.

User experience

One number, one shape, one colour. A glance can be all you need, and the depth is there when you want it.

UI design

The bar is drawn in the container background, so it stays put and glows through the Liquid Glass cards stacked above it.

Innovation

A game idea applied to health: not a ring to close or a score to chase, but a resource you spend and refill all day.

Accessibility

VoiceOver labels and values on the bar and every chart, Dynamic Type throughout, and haptics you can switch off.

Localization

Nine languages, with a true right-to-left Arabic build where the bar itself fills from the right.

Privacy

Health data comes from Apple Health. A share code shows a friend the percentage and never the stats behind it.

01 — THE GLANCE

Like the health bar in a game.

You already know how to read one. Full is rested. Low means you have been spending. The whole idea arrives in the first second, before anything is asked of you.

92%

Rested and recovered

Drag the bar. The zone colours and these four states are the app's own, not an illustration of them.

  • Real time, with a memory. It reacts to what you are doing now, while weighing your longer-term stats and your own baseline.
  • Always there, never in the way. On the watch face, in the Smart Stack, on the Lock Screen, or one Siri question away.
  • Depth when you want it. Heart rate, steps, calories, HRV, cardio fitness, respiratory rate and wrist temperature, each with a plain-language read.

03 — DESIGN

The bar glows through the glass.

A custom interaction layers all your health stats above the health bar. The bar is painted into the container background rather than into a card, so it holds its place and lights the Liquid Glass from behind as you explore.

  • Your day as a curve. Then a 30-day calendar underneath it. Tap any day for that day's high and low.
  • Charts you can touch. Scrub a chart and it reports the value under your finger, with a usual-range band behind it so a normal day looks normal.
  • Tags and analysis. Complex data turned into simple words you can act on, in a sentence, not a dashboard.
  • Turn the phone sideways and the bar goes full screen. Nothing else, just the bar.
  • Haptics and animation on every change of state, with a setting to turn the haptics off.

Always there, never in the way.

The app is the place you go when you want more. Most days you never open it, because the bar has already found you.

05 — MADE FOR EVERYONE

A bar anyone can read.

A visual is only simple if it is simple for everybody. The bar is labelled, it is sized by your text setting, and it is built again for right-to-left rather than flipped.

  • VoiceOver. The bar, every stat card and every chart carry a label and a spoken value, so the whole screen reads as sentences instead of numbers.
  • Dynamic Type. Layouts are measured in scaled metrics, so the bar and its labels grow with your text size rather than clipping.
  • Colour is never alone. Every zone states its percentage inside the bar, so the reading survives without the hue.
  • Haptics, optional. The bar can tell you with a tap instead of a glance, and the setting to silence it is one switch.
  • Your health data stays yours. Stats come from Apple Health. A share code carries the percentage and nothing underneath it, uses Sign in with Apple, and expires in about a day.

07 — ABOUT THE DEVELOPER

Bryce Ellis.

A Black solo iOS developer and marathoner, self-taught since 2017.

Bryce started with Udemy courses as a freshman computer science major and shipped the first IRL Health Bar in 2023. Everything on this page — the app on iPhone, the app on Apple Watch, the widgets, the complications, the nine localizations and the backend behind the share codes — is his own work.

The reason the app looks the way it does is that he wanted his own family to be able to read it. That test, more than any other, decided what stayed in.
